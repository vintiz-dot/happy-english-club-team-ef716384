# Speaking & Writing Lab (24 Nov 2026)

The owner's aim for Venus (Oxford Discover 1), SKY 1 (Oxford Discover 3) and Jupiter
(Reach Higher 3), and for every Oxford Discover 1-3 class: children who speak fluently
and automatically, and who write well. Every lesson of those courses now carries:

- **one fluency routine** (8-10 min): the lesson's grammar said as whole sentences,
  again and again, a little faster each round, with phrases from earlier units mixed back in;
- **one writing routine** (4-10 min): dictogloss, sentence combining, expand it, fix it,
  or model-then-write, always ending with a spoken step (read it, then say it without looking);
- about **every other lesson, a recorded talk** for home: the class routine repeated days
  later, recorded in "Record my video", scored on a short rubric the teacher approves.

Room is made by shortening games, warm-ups and repeated drills. Book pages, grammar,
checks, tests and homework are never cut (the code refuses to). How it is built:
`AGENTS.md`, section "Speaking & Writing Lab".

## What the research says, and how the Lab uses it

A deep-research run on 24 Nov 2026 searched the literature, read the sources and had
three independent checkers try to refute each claim. Below: what survived, how sure
it is, and what the Lab does with it.

### 1. Correct spoken grammar during speaking practice. Prompt the child to fix it. (high confidence)
- Oral corrective feedback in classrooms has significant effects that last to later
  testing (Lyster & Saito 2010, meta-analysis of 15 classroom studies, N = 827). Effects
  were largest on free speaking. Avoiding correction to protect fluency is not supported.
- **Prompts beat recasts**: making the child produce the correct form (start the phrase
  and pause, repeat the error with a rising voice, a clue such as "Past?") had large
  effects; the teacher simply saying it correctly (a recast) had medium effects.
- Younger learners appear to benefit **more** from correction, and more from prompts
  (medium confidence; the youngest group studied averaged 10-12, so SKY 1 and Jupiter
  more than Venus). Learners also tend to want more correction than teachers give.

**In the Lab:** every fluency slide has a teacher-only "How to correct" note for that
unit's grammar: prompts for grammar, a quick recast for a sound. Correct one or two
target structures, not everything.

### 2. Fluency comes from repeating the same speaking task. (medium confidence)
- Retelling the same story three times improved fluency and accuracy in 40 child EFL
  learners (Sun & Révész 2021).
- The 4/3/2 activity (the same talk to three new listeners in less time each round)
  raised speaking speed and cut pausing, with gains carried to a new topic, in
  **Vietnamese** EFL learners (Tran & Saito 2021). Repeating at a constant time did not
  carry over. The shrinking time matters.

**In the Lab:** routines 4-3-2, retell, task repeat, chunk sprint and question chain. The
times are shortened for children (Venus 60/45/30 s, SKY 1 and Jupiter 120/90/60 s); that
scaling is our inference, untested with children this young.

### 3. Repetition alone builds fluency, not accuracy. Fix errors between the first and second telling. (medium confidence)
- Time-pressured repetition on its own did not improve grammar. A one-minute review of
  the errors heard, between the first and second telling, improved accuracy (d = 1.04,
  and 1.42 on a new topic) and kept the fluency gains (Tran & Saito 2021; small sample).

**In the Lab:** every 4-3-2 and retell has the step "after round 1, stop for one minute,
put two mistakes on the board, fix them together, then go on". Chunk sprints and
question chains end with the same one-minute fix.

### 4. Spread the repetitions out. (low confidence)
- Six tellings in a row slowed speech and made children repeat word for word; spacing
  them reduced this (Suzuki & Hanzawa 2022, adults).

**In the Lab:** at most three rounds per routine. Earlier units' phrases come back in
"From earlier units", and the recorded talk repeats the class routine at home days later.

### 5. In writing, correct a few targets with a short rule. (low confidence)
- Focused feedback on two or three error types with a brief explanation did better than
  marking everything; codes alone were fixed "mechanically" and did not last (Deng et al.
  2022; Schenck 2020).

**In the Lab:** each writing slide has a checklist of 2-4 things a child can check, and
"fix it" sets target the class's real slips: plural and third-person -s, past -ed,
articles, "be", "do" in questions.

### What this run could not verify
Nothing survived checking on explicit vs implicit grammar teaching, processing
instruction, input flood, sentence combining, SRSD, dictogloss, games and songs, or
Vietnamese first-language transfer. The Lab still uses sentence combining, modelled
writing, dictogloss and a plan-write-check strategy. They are standard, widely
recommended practice for children's writing (for example the Writing Next review,
Graham & Perin 2007), but that is background knowledge, not something this research
re-checked. The same goes for the attention to final -s, which comes from teachers'
experience with Vietnamese learners.

Most verified studies are with adults or children of 10-12. Evidence for 6-8-year-olds
(Venus) is thin, so watch what works in Venus and adjust.

## Teacher's guide: running the routines

**Fluency (on screen: the round buttons start their own timer; a bell rings at the end)**
1. Show the "Say it fast and smooth" phrases; say them once together.
2. Round 1. Listen and do not stop anyone. Note two grammar mistakes (no names).
3. One minute: write the two mistakes on the board, the class fixes them, everyone says the fixed sentence.
4. Rounds 2 and 3 with new partners, less time. The listener has a job (count, tick, "Wow!").
5. Prompt, don't tell: start the phrase and pause; point to the frame; a finger for the missing -s.

**Grammar drill (every class, every lesson)**
Venus and Sanrio Grade 6 first; SKY 1 and Jupiter since 24 Nov 2026, with the Level 4 steps below.
1. Say a sentence and show a sign: ? (ask it), X (say it with not), + (add and / but / because ...), T (change the time), P (change the person).
2. The class says the new sentence together; then pairs with sign cards; swap at the clap.
3. One-minute fix after round 1, as in every routine.
4. Round 3 is real: the children ask about their own things and lives with the form they just drilled.

**Level 4 steps (SKY 1 and Jupiter, owner, 24 Nov 2026)**
The owner asked for SKY 1 (Oxford Discover 3) to be ready for Oxford Discover 4's grammar, and for Jupiter (Reach Higher 3) to match SKY 1. **Writing**
- Dictogloss: press "Read it aloud" twice (or read it yourself); groups rebuild the text; open "Show the text" only at the end.
- Combine / expand / fix: the model answers are folded under "Model answers (teacher)".
- Finish with the "Say it" step: the sentence aloud, then without looking.

**Paragraphs (every class, at least half the lessons)**
Venus (Level 1, grades 2-4) writes four to six sentences with joining words, planned with key words; Sanrio Grade 6 (Level 2, grades 6-7), SKY 1 and Jupiter write a full paragraph.
1. Read the model paragraph together; children point to the topic sentence, the details and the closing.
2. Plan in the boxes with a few words each (not sentences).
3. Write the paragraph from the plan, with the frames for Support.
4. Check with the checklist; a partner underlines two things the checklist asks for.
5. Mark only two targets, with a dot in the margin of the line; the child finds and fixes the mistake.
6. Say it: read it to a partner, then tell it without looking. It can become the recorded talk.

**The weekly talk**
1. In class, the talk slide (after the homework) shows the task, the practice steps and what is scored.
2. In Lesson Decks, after taking attendance, press **Set talk in app**: the talk is posted as video homework for the children present, with its rubric already approved.
3. Children practise three times and record once in "Record my video". You review each video's AI report and approve it; families see nothing before you do.
